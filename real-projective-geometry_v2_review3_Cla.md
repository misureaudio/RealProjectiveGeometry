**Confirmed error — immersion vs. embedding conflated (§6.3):**
The text claims "$\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^n$ embeds in $\mathbb{R}^{2n-\alpha(n)}$, where $\alpha(n)$ is the number of 1's in the binary expansion of $n$." That dimension bound belongs to the **immersion** conjecture (Massey's conjecture, proved by Ralph Cohen in 1985), not embedding. I checked this directly: the classical conjecture (going back to R. Brown, 1971) is that every $n$-manifold *immerses* in $\mathbb{R}^{2n-\alpha(n)}$ and *embeds* in $\mathbb{R}^{2n-\alpha(n)+1}$ — and even that embedding half isn't a proven theorem for all $n$, just a conjecture matching known sharp cases. So the document silently upgrades a hard-won immersion result into a stronger embedding claim it doesn't support. This is the kind of subtle swap an LLM makes because "immersion" and "embedding" pattern-match to the same neighborhood of facts.

**Likely error — geodesic intersection claim (§5.1):**
"Two distinct geodesics intersect in exactly one point — the projective analogue of the Euclidean axiom that two distinct lines intersect in exactly one point" is only true in $\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^2$ (where any two projective lines meet in a point, dimension-counting: $1+1-2=0$). For $\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^n$ with $n\geq 3$, two generic lines (geodesics) don't meet at all — that's exactly why higher-dimensional projective/incidence geometry needs Grassmannians and flats of controlled dimension to guarantee intersections. The document states this as a general fact about $\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^n$, which overgeneralizes a $\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^2$-only fact.

**Worth double-checking — cross-ratio symmetry group (§2.2):**
